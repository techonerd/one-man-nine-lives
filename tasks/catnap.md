# Task: Catnap (lock screen)

**Status: phase 1 done (still wallpaper); phase 2 (animated) open, see below.** Plasma 6 always loads the lock screen of the desktop shell (`org.kde.plasma.desktop`); a lock screen shipped in a global theme or a separate shell only loads in `kscreenlocker_greet --testing`. So Catnap does not replace the lock-screen QML.

- New art (for example from the media task) replaces `make_mono_sleeping_cat()` / `make_mono_bg()`; keep the cat in the lower-right corner.

## Phase 2: animated Catnap

*Casper asleep in the corner: slow, slightly uneven breathing, and now and then a lazy flick of the tail, as a cat does when it dreams.* An agent task (Antigravity does the work; Claude Code runs `plasma-polish review tasks/catnap.md` before the owner commits). Read `AGENTS.md`, `tasks/README.md` and the section above first.

Owner's brief (2026-11-24): about a 4 s rhythm, natural and calm; the smallest possible CPU and RAM footprint, and images loaded smartly; fewer frames if CPU is high.

### Why not an animated WebP or GIF
Plasma 6.7's image wallpaper can play them, but it repaints the **whole screen image** every frame:

| Approach (2560×1600 screen) | Image work per second | Extra RAM | Natural (non-repeating) tail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Animated WebP, 4 s loop at 12 fps (48 frames) | ~188 MB/s decoded, converted and uploaded to the GPU | ~47 MB (decoder canvas + frame + texture) | No: the tail flicks every 4 s, like a metronome. A 14 s loop needs 168 full frames |
| **Casper in parts, moved by QML (this task)** | **0 MB/s**: images load once; each frame only moves a few textures | **~2 MB** (1 MB background + ~0.7 MB parts) | Yes: timing is random, so it never repeats |

On a 3840×2400 screen a full WebP frame is 35 MB, so the first approach gets worse on exactly the screens that need it most. Doorstep's cat already uses the parts approach.

### The motion, in numbers
Casper's size comes from the current art: body about 211 px tall and tail about 160 px on a 2560×1600 screen (×0.68 at 1080p, ×1.5 on 4K-class screens).

**Breathing** (always on)
- One breath every **4.0 s ± 0.3 s**, a new random length each breath (3.7–4.3 s, about 14–16 breaths a minute: the calm end of a sleeping cat's usual range). A perfectly even rhythm looks mechanical.
- Each breath: **inhale 1.6 s** (eased), **exhale 1.9 s** (eased), then **rest** for the remainder (about 0.5 s).
- The body rises by **2.5 %** of its height (5 px at 2560×1600, 8 px at 4K), scaling from the floor line so the cat doesn't float. The head follows at 60 % of that rise, 150 ms later.
- Fastest movement: 5 px/s at 2560×1600, 8 px/s at 4K. At **8 updates a second** each step is under 1 px, which looks continuous. That is all breathing needs.

**Tail flick** (now and then)
- The tail is still most of the time. Every **10–18 s** (random), it flicks once:
  - It moves out over **0.9 s** and back over **1.1 s**, both eased.
  - It's drawn as three parts (base, middle, tip) that turn **3°, 6° and 10°**. Each part starts **120 ms** after the one before it, so a wave runs to the tip.
  - About one flick in four gets a second, smaller one (half the angle) 0.4 s after the first.
- The tip travels about 28 px at 2560×1600 (42 px at 4K), peaking at 49 px/s (73 px/s at 4K). At 30 updates a second each step is 1.6 px (2.4 px at 4K); at 12 it would be 4–6 px and visibly jerky. So the tail gets **30 updates a second, only during the 2 s flick**.
- The ears, eyes (closed) and everything else stay still. No "Zzz" and nothing else on screen moves.

**Updates per second, on average**
- **Normal:** 8 + (30 − 8) × 2 s / 14 s ≈ **11 updates a second**, below the 12 fps you suggested, and smoother where it counts.
- **Low-power fallback** (if CPU measures high): breathing at 6, flick at 20 ≈ **8 a second**. Breathing stays under 1.3 px per step at 4K; the tail goes to 3.7 px per step at 4K (slightly less smooth). Next step down: flicks every 20–30 s.
- **Still:** if System Settings → Animation speed is "Instant" (`Kirigami.Units.longDuration === 0`), Casper doesn't move at all.

### Build
- **Package**: turn `catnap/` into a Plasma 6 wallpaper plugin. Keep the Id `com.github.techonerd.catnap`, add `"KPackageStructure": "Plasma/Wallpaper"` and `"X-Plasma-API-Minimum-Version": "6.0"` to `metadata.json`, and write `contents/ui/main.qml` with a `WallpaperItem` root. Public APIs only. Update `install.sh`/`uninstall.sh` for its new place (`~/.local/share/plasma/wallpapers/`).
- **Art, drawn by code**: extend `look-and-feel/one-man-nine-lives/tools/generate_lockscreen_art.py` to write `contents/images/background.svg` and the sleeping-Casper parts: `body.svg`, `head.svg`, `tail-base.svg`, `tail-mid.svg`, `tail-tip.svg`. Each part needs a named pivot (shoulder of the tail, floor line of the body), documented the way `art/casper-parts/README.md` documents Doorstep's parts. The pose and colours stay as they are now (white coat `#F5F5F5`, no outline on dark, palette from Tabby & Tweed). The still `screenshot.png` stays.
- **One clock, no vsync animations**: a single `Timer` drives everything, with interval **125 ms** normally and **33 ms** while a flick runs. Each tick computes every angle and scale from elapsed time (`Date.now()`), so a late tick never slows Casper down. Don't use `NumberAnimation`, `Behavior` or `SequentialAnimation`: they run at the screen's refresh rate (60+ updates a second) and would cost 5–7 times more.
- **Smart loading**:
  - Every `Image` gets `asynchronous: true` and `cache: true`. The cache shares textures between screens when source and size match.
  - Parts are drawn once at their on-screen size: `sourceSize` = displayed size × `Screen.devicePixelRatio`. Nothing is drawn larger and scaled down.
  - The background is two near-identical greys plus a faint beam. Render it at **a quarter of the screen size** (`sourceSize` = screen ÷ 4: 1 MB rather than 16 MB) and let it scale up. Check the preview for banding; if any shows, use ÷ 2.
  - Don't use `AnimatedImage`, `Canvas`, `layer.enabled`, shaders, blur or `MultiEffect`.
- **Stop when no one can see it**: the Timer runs only while the window is visible and motion is allowed. When it stops, nothing is redrawn.
- **Testability**: expose `property real clockMs` (normally driven by the Timer) and `property bool still`, so tests and previews can set any moment exactly.
